Always log your entire configuration when booting

October 15, 2026 One-minute read
Programming • Observability • Provisioning

Here is a small tip that has saved me a lot of time: Always log your entire parsed configuration when your service or application boots. Or at least, support logging it through a verbose logging level. In practice, this can be logging the entire parsed YAML structure1, your config struct, or all the variables you have set from environment variables. No matter how you load your configuration, log it (but mask your secrets).

This habit has helped me more than once. If my application is failing to start, the first thing I do is sanity-check that my configuration has been read and parsed properly.

Say my CI/CD pipeline generates a YAML config file from AWS Config, stores it as a ConfigMap in Kubernetes, and then modifies my Kubernetes Deployment. A typo can slip in at any step. If my parsed application configuration can be checked through logs, I can quickly assess whether there is a bug in my application or in my CI/CD deployment pipeline.

Do yourself a favor and log your parsed configuration on boot. You will thank me later.

  1. Don’t log your raw YAML. If you do, your might have a bug in your application where you parse and/or extract your YAML into variables (or a struct). ↩︎
